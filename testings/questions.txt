Here are some questions that illustrate the difference between **vector RAG** and **vectorless (keyword/BM25) RAG**.

### Good semantic (vector RAG) questions

These intentionally avoid the exact legal wording.

1. **"Can I run electricity and water pipes through my neighbor's property if there is no other practical way to connect my house?"**

   * The law uses terms like **"lay pipe or line"** and **"basic services"**, not "run electricity" or "connect my house."
   * A vector search can associate these concepts even without exact keyword matches.

2. **"What happens if my usual route to the main road disappears after a landslide?"**

   * The text mentions **"passage... destroyed subsequently due to a disaster"** and **"servitude of a reasonable passage."**
   * The question uses "usual route" and "landslide" instead of the document's wording.

3. **"If emergency responders need to enter private property to save people during a flood, can they do so without permission?"**

   * The statute says **"victims may be rescued... by entering the house or land"** and lists disasters like **fire, earthquake, volcano and flood**.
   * The question paraphrases "entering the house or land" as "emergency responders need to enter private property."

4. **"Can a landowner block a pathway that the local community has used for generations?"**

   * The document refers to land **"in use since ancient time as a public or community road"**, not "used for generations."
   * Semantic retrieval is likely to connect these ideas.

5. **"When selling part of my land, do I have to make sure both the buyer and I can still get in and out?"**

   * The statute talks about **"provision of passage or exit"**, while the question uses everyday language.

---

### Questions vectorless RAG would likely answer easily

These contain the exact legal terminology.

* "What does Section 375 say about basic services?"
* "What is the servitude of passage under Section 373?"
* "What does Section 372 state about disasters?"
* "What does Section 376 say about using a river or stream?"

These match the wording in the document closely, making keyword search very effective.

---

### A particularly good demo question

If you want to demonstrate the advantage of vector embeddings, I'd use this:

> **"My house cannot be connected to electricity, water, or sewerage through my own property. Can I legally use my neighbor's land to install the necessary utilities?"**

This is a strong test because:

* It never uses the exact phrase **"lay pipe or line."**
* It paraphrases **"basic services"** as **"utilities."**
* It asks the legal principle in natural language rather than quoting the statute.

A semantic embedding model should retrieve Section 375 because it understands the meaning, whereas a purely keyword-based retriever has a higher chance of missing it if the keywords don't align well.
